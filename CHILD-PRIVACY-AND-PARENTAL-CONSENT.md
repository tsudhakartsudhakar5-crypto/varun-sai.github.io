# Child Privacy and Parental Consent: author website
**The website is not fully compliant with the children's-data rules while a verified parental-consent process is missing.** The interim design avoids needing one by not inviting children's personal data.

## 1. Reading vs. sending personal data
- **Reading** every public page (home, about, books, articles, quotes, announcements, polls) needs no account and no details. Children can do this. As on any site, hosting and database providers see technical data such as the IP address. The site runs no ads, analytics or tracking.
- **Sending** a contact message or feedback collects personal data (name, email, message). These forms are for **adults (18+)**. Under the DPDP Act a "child" is anyone under 18.
- **Voting** collects no name or email, only a random browser code. Whether that is "personal data" and whether children may vote is a question for the lawyer; the site currently lets anyone vote.

## 2. Interim restriction (what was built)
Both forms now require a tick-box: "I have read the privacy information and I am 18 or older", with a notice telling under-18s to ask a parent or guardian to write for them. The privacy policy and terms say the same.
**Limitation:** a tick-box cannot prove age and is not parental consent. A child can tick it. It is a reminder and declaration only, and it is not stored. If a lawyer says it is not enough, the safest temporary step is to remove the two forms and keep the email address only.

## 3. Before children could send data
From the Act and Rules as described by commentary and PIB (check the Gazette text):
1. **Verifiable parental consent** before processing a child's personal data (s.9(1), Rule 10): checking the parent is an identifiable adult, for example through reliable identity or age details already on record or a virtual token from a government-authorised service. A tick-box does not do this.
2. For a person with a disability and a lawful guardian: verified guardian consent (Rule 11).
3. **No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children** (s.9(3)); no processing likely to harm a child's well-being (s.9(2)).
4. A way for the parent to withdraw consent, and erasure on request.
I have not found an exemption that fits an author website: do not assume one.

## 4. Dates
Section 9 and Rules 10 and 11 are stated to begin about **13 May 2027**. Until then this is preparation, but design for it now.

## 5. If a child's data arrives
Delete it promptly, log it (retention plan), and reply to the parent if they write.
